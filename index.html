CSP Decline Intelligence · Product Concept

Closing the intelligence gap
between the CSP and Wiom

Every day CSPs tell us why they won't install a booking — and we throw the signal away. Not because it's worthless, but because we can't trust it: we don't know if the CSP picked the reason thoughtfully, and we don't know if the list even covers their real reason. This is a plan to fix the input, help the CSP decide well, verify what they claim, and feed every confirmed signal back into the system — a self-learning loop.

Builds on: the CSP decline analysis (Jun–Aug 2026) Data: ES install candidates + transition log · last 30–45 days Status: concept for review
The idea in one picture

A decline is not an endpoint — it's a signal to learn from

Today the loop dead-ends at "capture". The goal is to carry every decline all the way around: capture it well, help the CSP decide, verify the claim, act on it, and learn back into routing, serviceability and quality.

1 · Capture

Better reasons

A cleaner, unbiased, more complete reason set the CSP actually engages with.

2 · Assist

Informed decision

At the moment of decline, show the CSP what we know so they choose the right option.

3 · Verify

Close the trust gap

Check the claim — against our own data, or by asking the customer directly.

4 · Act

Route / cancel / hold

Re-route to a new CSP, stop routing, or cancel — based on the verified truth.

5 · Learn

Feed the system

Send confirmed signals to Genie serviceability, Quality, and the reason taxonomy.

↻The self-learning loop: stage 5 rewrites the inputs to stages 1–4 — serviceability maps get sharper, CSP profiles get truer, and the reason list evolves from what CSPs actually type. The system gets less wrong every week.
Why we do nothing today

Two confidence gaps make the signal unusable

1 · Did the CSP choose thoughtfully?

The reason sits at the top of a fixed list, one tap away from "done". We can't tell a considered answer from a reflex tap — so we can't act on any single decline as fact. ~65% of declines are one reason ("service not available"), which is exactly what position-bias would produce.

2 · Does the list even cover the truth?

Six fixed options, no free-text, no "other". If the real reason is "I couldn't find the address", the CSP is forced into the nearest wrong box. We never learn what the list is missing, so it never improves.

Part 1 · Fix the input

Redefine the reason set

Before we can trust a reason, the menu has to be clean, unbiased, and complete. Four changes.

Remove — redundant

"Network setup is currently not possible"

This is a coverage/backhaul claim — the same physical fact as "service not available". Making the CSP choose between two coverage reasons adds guesswork, not signal. Fold it into Service not available; recover the finer distinction downstream from the serviceability check, not from the CSP's tap. Coverage then becomes ~78% of declines — and Part 2's coverage intervention covers even more.

Club — same intent, same action

"Customer refused"

"Not interested" (7.4%) and "didn't agree with price" (3.4%) are the same signal — the customer doesn't want it — and both trigger the exact same next step (verify with the customer). Splitting them adds no downstream value and dilutes the count. One reason: Customer refused, with an optional detail chip for price.

Add — a real gap

"Could not understand the address"

Address quality has no box today, so it hides inside coverage and customer reasons. It's a distinct, fixable cause — the fix is address correction, not de-serviceability. Capturing it cleanly stops it from poisoning the coverage signal.

Add — let them tell us

"Other — tell us more"

A free-text catch-all. This is the engine of the self-learning loop's Part 1: what CSPs type into "Other" is the evidence for the next reason we should add. The list stops being frozen and starts evolving from reality.

Randomise the order — every time

"Service not available" is always first and gets ~65% of taps — the shape of position bias, not necessarily of reality. Shuffle the reason order on every render (keeping "Other" pinned last). Over thousands of declines the true rates surface, and no single reason gets a free ride to the top. This is the cheapest way to buy back confidence in gap #1.

Part 2 · Help the CSP decide well

Informed decline — reason by reason

A cleaner list still relies on the CSP choosing correctly. So at the moment of decline, for the reasons that matter most, we interrupt with what we already know and let them make an informed call. We take the two biggest reasons one at a time.

Reason A · ~65% of all declines

"Service not available at this location"

The single biggest reason — and the one we trust least. When a CSP says a spot isn't serviceable, we have no idea if it's true or a reflex. But we often already have active connections a few doors away. So we show them.

What each answer buys us

"Let me re-check"
Decline avoided

The CSP reconsiders — a false decline never happens, the booking keeps its best CSP.

"Still not serviceable"
High-confidence signal

A decline made despite seeing nearby live connections is worth 100× a reflex tap. This is gold for Part 3.

Either way we win. We either save a good booking, or we turn an untrusted tap into a deliberate, high-confidence coverage claim.

Reason B · ~11% of declines · the customer-intent bucket

"Customer refused"

When CSP #1 says the customer refused, today we don't validate it — we just re-offer to CSP #2. If #1 was telling the truth, we've now sent CSP #2 on a wild-goose chase and burned their experience for a dead booking. The fix: ask the customer before we route onward.

Customer verification loop — three branches

Customer: "Yes, I don't want it"
▣ Cancel & stop routing

CSP #1 was right. Cancel the booking, stop re-offering, fast-refund. No other CSP wastes a visit. → Part 3

Customer: "No, I do want it"
▶ Keep routing + flag the CSP

The booking is alive — routing continues normally. But CSP #1's claim was false: send a signal to Quality to profile them. → Part 3

Customer: no response
◷ As usual

No new information — fall back to today's behaviour and re-offer. We lose nothing.

Part 3 · Close the loop

Feed every confirmed signal back into the system

This is what makes it self-learning. Each verified signal from Parts 1–2 has a home — a system that gets smarter because of it.

→ Genie

Serviceability influence

High-confidence coverage declines (made after seeing nearby live connections) and their opposite (CSPs who backed off when shown) flow to Genie. Where many independent CSPs confirm "not serviceable despite neighbours", Genie sharpens the serviceability map so we stop generating offers we can't fulfil. Where they back off, the area stays open. The 65% coverage bucket becomes a live feedback channel, not a graveyard.

→ Quality

CSP truthfulness profile

When the customer verification loop proves a CSP's "customer refused" was false, that's a durable fact about the CSP. Quality accumulates it: a CSP who repeatedly makes false customer claims gets profiled and down-weighted. Honest declines cost nothing; false ones build a record.

→ Taxonomy

The reason list evolves itself

Everything typed into "Other" is mined for recurring themes. When a theme crosses a threshold, it graduates into a first-class reason — and a rarely-chosen reason gets retired. The menu in Part 1 stops being a one-time redesign and becomes a living list that tracks reality.

The loop, closed

Sharper serviceability (Genie) means fewer bad offers reach a CSP — so fewer coverage declines to capture next month. Truer CSP profiles (Quality) mean better routing — so fewer false "customer refused" claims. A living taxonomy means the next decline is captured more precisely. Stage 5 rewrites stages 1–4. That's the self-learning loop.

Sequencing

How this rolls out

Each part ships value on its own; together they compound. Build in this order.

  1. Reason-set redesign + randomisation Part 1 — app-side change to the decline/failure sheet: remove, club, add two reasons, shuffle order, add free-text "Other". Ships confidence immediately; needs no backend intelligence.
  2. Coverage interrupt — "nearby active connections" Part 2A — the popup on "service not available", reading existing active-connection geodata. Highest-volume reason, biggest single win.
  3. Customer verification loop Part 2B — automated customer callback/WhatsApp on "customer refused", with the three-branch outcome. Reuses the verification channel already scoped in the decline plan.
  4. Signal pipes to Genie & Quality Part 3 — wire the verified outputs into serviceability and CSP profiling. This is where the loop closes and the compounding begins.
  5. Taxonomy mining from "Other" Part 3 — periodic review of free-text to evolve the reason list. Lowest urgency, highest long-term leverage.
