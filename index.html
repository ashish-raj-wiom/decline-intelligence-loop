Decline Intelligence · one-pager for leadership

Send each CSP only the bookings
he'll actually install

Turn every decline reason into a self-learning loop — capture the reason in context, send a clean signal to the service that owns that problem, and let it fix the root cause. Each reason is a roadmap item; we start by cleaning up the reason list itself.

Spine: Show → Assist/Verify & Capture → Emit Signal → Analyse & Build Consequence First live: Location is Far (≈65% of declines) · go-live 19 Aug Status: for leadership review

Problem

When a CSP declines a booking he tells us why — but today we throw that reason away and re-offer blindly, often to the wrong CSP again. Bookings bounce through re-offers until they time out, and the customer waits days to get online.

Objective

Send only quality, serviceable bookings to each CSP — automatically, by learning from every decline. Each reason feeds the service that can fix it, so the wrong bookings stop arriving.

Guardrail

This layer only captures & emits signals — it never routes, cancels, refunds or de-lists. The owning service (Genie, Customer backend, Ops…) acts. Only data the CSP was shown is used, and he is never blocked from declining.

Context · what a decline is

When a CSP won't install, he tells us why

A booking sent to a CSP can be turned down at two moments — a decline before he accepts, or an install-failure report after — and at both he picks a reason from the same fixed list. That reason is the signal this whole exercise is built on. First, the lay of the land.

The two drop-offs we act on

Of every task created, the CSP declines it, accepts and installs, or accepts and reports a failure. The two drops where he gives us a reason — and which this project acts on — are these:

go-live 19 Aug Location is Far — the full four steps ›

Signed off on both sides — the CSP-side PRD and the Genie PRD.

1
Show

Show the reason list this layer

Step 1 shows the CSP the fixed list of decline reasons and lets him pick. What matters: which reasons are visible, that they read as neutral options, and that their order is randomised so a reason isn't chosen just for sitting on top. He taps the one that fits — here, "Location is Far". Nothing is interpreted yet; that starts at Step 2.

More →

The finalised six reasons, and what was kept / removed / added, are in the signed-off Reason Set PRD.

2
Assist / Verify & Capture

Make the decline deliberate — and capture the points he's rejecting this layer

Before the decline is finalised we show the CSP what we already know: he has active connections a few doors away. That assist is the verification — our own data is already in front of him, so there's no Ops-calling layer for this reason. If he backs off, a false decline never happens. If he still declines, we capture two things — the deliberate decision ("I won't serve here") and the exact points we showed him, in order, that he's rejecting. Only a point he was actually shown can ever be sent on.

⚠︎ You already have 2 connections near here

This new connection is only 40 m away — the cable won't be long:

● Ram Kumar · B-12 Saket Ext 40 m
● Preeti Sharma · A-8 Neb Sarai 200 m
I won't be able to connect
OK, I'll connect

Illustration — exact copy & flow live in Figma / the CSP PRD.

What we record

"OK, I'll connect" → decline avoided. The booking stays with him; no signal sent. The back-off is logged for measurement.

"I won't be able to connect" → a deliberate decline. We record it and the points shown, in order, as the ones he rejects — and the booking is removed with a confirmation.

Showing the neighbours turns a careless tap into a high-confidence claim — the cheapest false-decline filter there is, and no human check needed. If no points come back in time, the decline still completes but no signal is sent (no informed decision).

3
Emit Signal

Push the signal to Genie Genie owns it next

This is the scope boundary. We build no candidate list, route nothing, de-list no one. We push one clean, self-describing, point-level signal — "CSP X declined connection Y for Location is Far, after being shown these points, in this order" — to Genie. Pushed, not left in a table to poll. We're the system-of-record; Genie owns the decision.

How routing works: Genie builds each booking's candidate-CSP list by distance from every CSP's active points; DAS then routes among those candidates. We only feed Genie the signal that changes which points count — DAS is affected only indirectly.

4
Analyse & Build Consequence

Genie drops the point for that CSP Genie acts

Using the signal, Genie applies its own threshold: once a CSP's point (active or splitter) crosses the bar, Genie stops counting it in the distance calc for him. From then he isn't surfaced for new bookings near it — so DAS never routes them to him. The clutter is gone from his feed, and the booking goes to a CSP who will install. What the threshold is, and what "de-list" means, is Genie's to decide — out of scope of our PRDs.
