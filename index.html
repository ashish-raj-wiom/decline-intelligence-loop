CSP Decline Intelligence · Scope 1 · one reason, end-to-end

Send each CSP only the bookings
he'll actually install

When a CSP says "service not available at this location" and declines, today we throw the signal away and re-offer the booking blindly — often to the wrong CSP, again. This turns that one reason into a self-learning loop: capture it in context, help the CSP avoid a careless decline, verify it's real, and let Genie stop routing that pocket to a CSP who won't serve it. The objective is simple — filter the clutter, so every CSP gets only quality, serviceable bookings.

The loop in one picture

Five steps, one reason, all the way around

Every step is scoped to a single reason — "service not available". The loop only counts as built if it runs end-to-end: from the CSP's tap to Genie actually changing who gets routed there.

Step 1 · Show

Reason in context

Surface the reason at the right moment, framed as neutral signal.

Step 2 · Assist & capture

Informed decline

Show what we know, let him decide, and capture the area he's rejecting.

Step 3 · Verify

Is it a false decline?

Test the claim against our own data and aligned-incentive ground truth.

Step 4 · Signal

Emit to Genie

Hand the verified signal to serviceability. We don't act — we inform.

Step 5 · Consequence

Genie stops routing

Past a threshold, Genie de-lists that pocket for that CSP.

↻The self-learning loop: once Genie de-lists a pocket for a CSP, no new booking from there reaches him — so the clutter he used to decline never arrives again. Fewer bad offers → fewer coverage declines → a cleaner supply map every month.
Why we're doing this

The objective, and how we'll know it worked

For this reason, the goal is to send only quality serviceable bookings to the CSP and filter out the clutter — automatically, by learning from every decline. Success is measured where it matters: the CSP's feed gets cleaner, and the customer gets installed faster.

How this helps the customer — the answer to "what happens after Step 5"

A customer in a genuinely-serviced pocket stops being routed to a CSP who'll refuse them. Their booking lands on a CSP who will install — first time — instead of bouncing through re-offers until it times out. The loop's payoff is not a cleaner dashboard; it's a customer who waits days less to get online.

Service not available · end to end

The one reason, through all five steps

1
Show

Capture the reason, in the right context this layer

Present "service not available at this location" as one clear, neutral option at the moment of decline — no penalty language, no friction. Randomise position so it isn't chosen just because it sits on top. The only job here is to record an honest, unbiased tap.

2
Assist & capture

Help him avoid a false decline — and capture the area he's rejecting this layer

Before we accept the decline, we show the CSP what we already know: he has active connections a few doors away. If he backs off, a false decline never happens. If he still declines, we capture two things — the decision (a deliberate "I won't serve here"), and the specific active points / pocket he is declaring un-serviceable. That pocket, not just the booking, is the unit we learn on.

⚠︎ Are you sure service isn't available here?

You have 3 active connections within 150 m of this address:

● House 42, same lane 40 m
● Green Apt, Block B 95 m
● Shop 7, main road 150 m
Let me re-check
Still not serviceable

What we record

"Let me re-check" → decline avoided. The booking keeps its best CSP. Nothing else happens.

"Still not serviceable" → a deliberate decline, made despite seeing live neighbours. We log it against the nearby active points as one flag: "this CSP won't serve this pocket."

Showing the neighbours is what converts a careless tap into a high-confidence claim. It's the cheapest false-decline filter we have — and it happens before Step 3 ever runs.

3
Verify

The verification layer — how we detect a false decline this layer

A single decline is never trusted or acted on. "False decline" here means the pocket is actually serviceable / installable, but this CSP said no. We test that claim from three independent angles and only accumulate confidence when they agree — a decline is acted on only when the evidence says it's real and repeated.

High conf

Aligned-incentive ground truth — Dominance calling

Ops already does follow-up calling on every Dominance CSP, who has a strong incentive to install (trending 75%+ SE). Their declines are near-ground-truth, and the calling flow can directly confirm whether a pocket was really un-serviceable. This is the calibration set that tells us what a true "not serviceable" looks like versus a brush-off. (Data source to be wired — currently behind login.)

Med conf

Cross-CSP contradiction

If another CSP later installs the same booking or an address in that pocket, the first CSP's "not serviceable" was false — the area is serviceable, just not by him. That's exactly the signal we want: don't route him here, but keep routing others.

Med conf

Own-data contradiction

The nearby active connections from Step 2, logged over time. Repeated deliberate declines against a pocket where the CSP already runs live connections is strong evidence the "not serviceable" is really "I won't go there".

The design question this answers

"How does this detect false negatives?" — by never trusting one tap, and by triangulating each pocket-level claim against aligned-incentive calling, cross-CSP installs, and our own connection map. Confidence accrues per pocket; only a corroborated, repeated claim graduates to Step 4.

4
Signal

Emit the verified signal to Genie Genie acts

This is the scope boundary. We do not change routing, de-list anyone, or touch the splitter map ourselves. We publish a clean, verified, pocket-level signal — "CSP X, ≥N deliberate not-serviceable flags for pocket P" — and Genie reads it. We are the trusted system-of-record; Genie owns the decision.

5
Consequence

Genie de-lists the pocket for that CSP Genie acts

Using the signal, Genie applies a threshold: once a CSP's active point / pocket crosses the flag bar, Genie removes it from that CSP's active-connection / splitter list. From then on, new bookings from that pocket never route to him — because we've learned he won't install there. The clutter is gone from his feed, and the booking goes to a CSP who will.

Threshold (>2) is illustrative — Genie owns the exact bar and can tune it per confidence source.

Bounds of this feature

We capture and expose. Other services read and act.

This is deliberately a capture + system-of-record layer, not a controller. It holds the reason taxonomy, the informed-decline surface, the pocket-level flags, and the verified signal — and publishes them. Every action belongs to the service that owns it.

Reason "Customer refused" — customer-side verification loop ›

When a CSP says the customer refused, today we don't validate it — we just re-offer to the next CSP, breaking their experience if the first CSP was right. The same spine applies, but the verification and the action live on the customer side, not here: this layer would only emit a "customer-refused, needs verification" signal, and the customer-side system owns the contact and the outcome.

Customer: "Yes, I don't want it"
▣ Cancel & stop routing

CSP was right. Customer-side cancels + fast-refunds; no other CSP wastes a visit.

Customer: "No, I do want it"
▶ Keep routing + record

Booking stays alive. The disproven claim is recorded for Quality to evaluate — never surfaced to the CSP as a strike.

Customer: no response
◷ As usual

No new information — fall back to today's behaviour. We lose nothing.

Scope 1 · sequencing

Build order for the one full loop

The rule: ship at least one reason end-to-end — input and feedback — not an input layer with no consequence.

  1. Capture in context + randomisation Step 1 — the neutral, unbiased "service not available" tap.
  2. Informed-decline surface + pocket capture Step 2 — nearby-connections popup, reading active-connection geodata; record the decision and the pocket.
  3. Verification layer Step 3 — accumulate pocket-level confidence from cross-CSP installs and own-data; wire Dominance calling as ground truth.
  4. Verified signal to Genie Step 4 — the published, pocket-level record Genie consumes.
  5. Genie consequence live Step 5 — threshold de-listing that changes who future bookings route to. This is what makes it a loop, not a UX change.
