When a CSP won't install a booking, he tells us why — and today we throw that reason away and re-offer blindly, often to the wrong CSP again. This turns every decline reason into a self-learning loop: show the reason in context, assist the CSP so his answer is deliberate, capture it, send a clean signal to the service that owns that problem, and let that service build the consequence. One spine, four steps, applied to every reason — and we've specced the biggest reason, "Location is Far", first, end-to-end (go-live expected 14 Aug).

Context · what a decline is

When a CSP won't install, he tells us why

A booking sent to a CSP can be turned down at two moments — a decline before he accepts, or an install-failure report after — and at both he picks a reason from the same fixed list. That reason is the signal this whole exercise is built on. First, the lay of the land.

The two drop-offs we act on

Of every task created, the CSP declines it, accepts and installs, or accepts and reports a failure. The two drops where he gives us a reason — and which this project acts on — are these:

The loop in one picture · any reason

Four steps, every reason, all the way around

Every decline reason runs the same spine. Verification isn't a separate step — for most reasons the assist (showing the CSP what we already know) is the check, so there's no Ops-calling layer for Location is Far. It only counts as built when it runs end-to-end: from the CSP's tap to the owning service building the consequence.

Step 1 · Show

Reason in context

Surface the fixed reason list at the decline / install-failure moment; the CSP picks the one that fits.

Step 2 · Assist / Verify & Capture

Make the answer deliberate

Show him what we already know, let him decide, verify by that assist (no Ops layer), and capture the decision + its data.

Step 3 · Emit Signal

Send to the owning service

Push one clean, self-describing signal to the service that owns that problem. We don't act — we inform.

Step 4 · Build Consequence

The owner acts

Past a threshold, the owning service changes behaviour so the CSP stops getting that kind of clutter.

↻The self-learning loop: each captured reason teaches the service that owns it, so the clutter the CSP used to decline stops arriving — fewer bad offers → fewer declines → a cleaner supply map every month.

Why we're doing this

The objective

For this reason, the goal is to send only quality serviceable bookings to the CSP and filter out the clutter — automatically, by learning from every decline. (Success metrics are at the end of the walkthrough below.)

How this helps the customer — the answer to "what happens after Step 4"

A customer in a genuinely-serviced area stops being sent to a CSP who'll refuse them. Their booking lands on a CSP who will install — first time — instead of bouncing through re-offers until it times out. The loop's payoff is not a cleaner dashboard; it's a customer who waits days less to get online.

Location is Far · the worked example

The one we've specced, through all four steps

Location is Far is signed off on both sides — the CSP-side PRD and the Genie PRD. Here it is end to end.

1
Show

Show the reason list this layer

Step 1 shows the CSP the fixed list of decline reasons and lets him pick. What matters: which reasons are visible, that they read as neutral options, and that their order is randomised so a reason isn't chosen just for sitting on top. He taps the one that fits — here, "Location is Far". Nothing is interpreted yet; that starts at Step 2.

More →

The full reason-set design (the finalised six, and what was kept / removed / added) is in the archive and the signed-off Reason Set PRD.

2
Assist / Verify & Capture

Make the decline deliberate — and capture the points he's rejecting this layer

Before the decline is finalised we show the CSP what we already know: he has active connections a few doors away. That assist is the verification — our own data is already in front of him, so there's no Ops-calling layer for this reason. If he backs off, a false decline never happens. If he still declines, we capture two things — the deliberate decision ("I won't serve here") and the exact points we showed him, in order, that he's rejecting. Only a point he was actually shown can ever be sent on.

⚠︎ You already have 2 connections near here

This new connection is only 40 m away — the cable won't be long:

● Ram Kumar · B-12 Saket Ext 40 m
● Preeti Sharma · A-8 Neb Sarai 200 m
I won't be able to connect
OK, I'll connect

Illustration — exact copy & flow live in Figma / the CSP PRD.

What we record

"OK, I'll connect" → decline avoided. The booking stays with him; no signal sent. The back-off is logged for measurement.

"I won't be able to connect" → a deliberate decline. We record it and the points shown, in order, as the ones he rejects — and the booking is removed with a confirmation.

Showing the neighbours turns a careless tap into a high-confidence claim — the cheapest false-decline filter there is, and no human check needed. If no points come back in time, the decline still completes but no signal is sent (no informed decision).

3
Emit Signal

Push the signal to Genie Genie owns it next

This is the scope boundary. We build no candidate list, route nothing, de-list no one. We push one clean, self-describing, point-level signal — "CSP X declined connection Y for Location is Far, after being shown these points, in this order" — to Genie. Pushed, not left in a table to poll. We're the system-of-record; Genie owns the decision.

How routing works: Genie builds each booking's candidate-CSP list by distance from every CSP's active points; DAS then routes among those candidates. We only feed Genie the signal that changes which points count — DAS is affected only indirectly.

4
Build Consequence

Genie drops the point for that CSP Genie acts

Using the signal, Genie applies its own threshold: once a CSP's point (active or splitter) crosses the bar, Genie stops counting it in the distance calc for him. From then he isn't surfaced for new bookings near it — so DAS never routes them to him. The clutter is gone from his feed, and the booking goes to a CSP who will install. What the threshold is, and what "de-list" means, is Genie's to decide — out of scope of our PRDs.

Reason "Customer refused" — customer-side verification loop ›

When a CSP says the customer refused, today we don't validate it — we just re-offer to the next CSP, breaking their experience if the first CSP was right. The same spine applies, but the verification and the action live on the customer side, not here: this layer would only emit a "customer-refused, needs verification" signal, and the customer-side system owns the contact and the outcome.

Customer: "Yes, I don't want it"
▣ Cancel & stop routing

CSP was right. Customer-side cancels + fast-refunds; no other CSP wastes a visit.

Customer: "No, I do want it"
▶ Keep routing + record

Booking stays alive. The disproven claim is recorded for Quality to evaluate — never surfaced to the CSP as a strike.

Customer: no response
◷ As usual

No new information — fall back to today's behaviour. We lose nothing.
